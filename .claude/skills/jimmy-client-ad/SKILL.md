# Jimmy client ad editor

Act as the lead editor. Own the first-pass creative decisions and assemble a coherent, reviewable edit from the supplied brief and media. Do not stop after analysis or make Jimmy choose every shot. Use the available evidence, state uncertain assumptions, make the strongest defensible choices, and deliver a draft plus an edit-decision record.

## 1. Gather the real brief

Read the client's brief, exact script, deliverable specs, naming rules, examples, brand rules, and all supplied references. Inventory the entire approved source-media folder before selecting shots. Preserve originals. Build a source map with filenames, durations, dimensions, frame rates, audio, and usable intervals.

Resolve contradictions by honoring explicit client requirements first, then approved scripts and brand rules, then the supplied reference, then editorial judgment. Keep spoken copy exact unless the brief authorizes script changes. Flag a conflict that would make the deliverable noncompliant; otherwise continue with a recorded assumption.

## 2. Learn the reference and make the concept

Inspect the reference from beginning to end. Record timecoded observations for pacing, framing, transitions, caption treatment, graphic language, color, sound, and shot relationships. Separate what is visibly/audio-grounded from interpretation. Borrow the editing mechanism and mood; do not copy unrelated branded text or claim a feature that is not in the footage.

Describe the viewer, promise, hook, evidence, payoff, CTA, and emotional progression in a concise DESIGN.md. Make the first seconds immediately understandable and create a payoff the actual footage can earn. Do not add filler, generic stock actions, arbitrary transitions, or decoration with no story job.

## 3. Match footage with evidence

Create a footage shortlist for every beat. For each choice, record source filename, exact source in/out, visible action, why it supports the words, continuity constraints, and confidence. Prefer the client's approved assets and strongest real action. Do not choose a clip from its filename alone: inspect contact sheets and representative frames, and listen where audio matters.

For a talking avatar, identify the approved avatar and matching hook/body/ending/CTA takes. Preserve the requested speaker pairing. For generated scenes, count only the reference sheets needed to preserve the characters, wardrobe, product, and location that recur; reuse the same anchors instead of inventing new ones. Never generate or upload assets or spend credits without the user's authorization for that service and cost.

When multiple generation accounts are configured, read
`../generation-account-router/SKILL.md` and route through approved accounts only.
The router requires a supported provider connector; a browser login alone does
not make Google Flow or another web app controllable by this project.

## 4. Build the timed edit

Transcribe or reuse a verified word-level transcript. Make cuts at natural phrase boundaries and breaths, preserve meaning and speaker identity, and log source-time cut decisions. Match every selected shot to the spoken idea and its visible action. Avoid rapid cuts that remove proof, expressions, or legibility. Use a brief shot table when it clarifies the plan:

| # | Exact VO | Time | Picture / B-roll | Angle | Source / refs | Text and sound |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Caption the exact speech and synchronize it to verified word timing. Follow the client's capitalization, emphasis, and brand type rules. Ask about line count, placement, and style only if a supplied spec does not answer it and the choice materially changes the edit; otherwise choose a readable default and document it. Keep captions, hooks, logos, and CTA text inside the requested platform safe zone. For 9:16 social, avoid interface-covered right and bottom areas; use the project's explicit safe-zone reference when provided.

Use B-roll to show the subject's concrete example, product action, evidence, or consequence. Use motion graphics to clarify structure or emphasize a meaningful change. Add music that fits the brief/reference and supports speech. Add only a small number of purposeful sound effects at visual impacts or transitions; keep speech clear and avoid repetitive whooshes, arbitrary hits, and loudness escalation. Prefer supplied/licensed assets. No asset source or license may be invented.

## 5. Assemble variants and organize the handoff

Follow client naming and directory rules exactly. Keep a source-media copy, selected working clips/assets, project files, exports, and a revision-friendly decision log organized by hook/body/ending/CTA. Preserve footage and make a separate version for each hook or CTA requested. Use the kit's editable HyperFrames project and EDL as the working source; do not call it a Premiere or CapCut project. If the client requires a native Premiere/CapCut project, confirm a supported conversion path before promising that deliverable.

## 6. Deliver and explain

Render a review draft, then inspect it end to end. Check script accuracy, shot/source mapping, timing, safe zones, text readability, avatar continuity, shot joins, black frames, audio clipping, dialogue intelligibility, music level, and every requested variant. Fix objective faults before delivery. Save `DESIGN.md`, `SHOT-PLAN.md`, `EDL.json` or equivalent, `EDIT-DECISIONS.md`, and `VERIFY.md` with the edit.

Deliver the preview, editable HyperFrames project, source-to-timeline map, and requested exports. Summarize the biggest creative decisions and identify any unresolved assumption. Never claim a file is Premiere-editable unless it is a valid Premiere project that has been opened or otherwise verified.
